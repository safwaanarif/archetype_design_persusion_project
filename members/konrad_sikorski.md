# Konrad Sikorski

**Archetype: The Hero**

## Why the AI suggested it

I worked through a set of questions with an AI assistant (Claude) instead of taking its first answer. When asked what drives me most, I picked *achieving goals and proving myself*, which narrows the field to the three achievement archetypes: the Hero, the Magician, and the Outlaw. When asked how I picture reaching my goals, I picked *beating the odds through hard work and grit*. That points to the Hero.

The Hero's core desire is to prove his worth through courage and effort. Its biggest fear is weakness or giving up, and its gift is determination.

The AI also connected it to my story. I'm the first in my family to go to college, and I'm working toward a career in IT at a big company. Nobody handed me a map for that, so I'm building the path myself.

## Do I agree?
Yes. The AI also raised the Magician as a possible fit, since that archetype suits a lot of people in IT: it's about using knowledge to transform things. But the Magician is about vision and making the impossible happen, while the Hero is about effort and not quitting. The second one describes how I actually get things done, so I'm going with the Hero.

## Suggested brand style

**Imagery:** Sunrise over a city skyline, someone mid-climb on a rock wall or staircase, an athlete training alone early in the morning, a clean server room or workstation at night. The theme is effort in progress, not the finish line.

**Colors:**
- Navy blue (`#1B2A4A`) for strength and reliability
- Bold red (`#C62828`) for courage and energy
- Gold (`#F2B632`) as an accent for achievement
- White for clean contrast

**Fonts:**
- Headings: *Oswald* or *Montserrat Bold*, strong, upright, and condensed
- Body text: *Inter* or *Roboto*, clean and easy to read

**Sample phrasing:**
- "Built, not given."
- "First in line, first to finish."
- "Every problem is a challenge I haven't beaten yet."
- "I don't wait for the path. I make it."

## Cialdini's principles applied to my brand

**Reciprocity:** Help classmates debug their setup or explain a Git command before asking for anything back. People remember who helped them when they were stuck, which builds goodwill for group projects and future job references.

**Commitment and consistency:** Publicly commit to goals, like posting on LinkedIn that I'm working toward a specific IT certification. Once I say it out loud, I'm more likely to follow through, and people start to see me as someone who finishes what he starts.

**Social proof:** Show real evidence of work: GitHub commits, finished projects, completed labs. A steady contribution history proves effort better than claiming to be hardworking.

**Authority:** Earn credibility through certifications (like CompTIA A+ or Network+) and hands-on experience. For a Hero brand, authority should come from proven skill, not a title.

**Liking:** Share the real story, including being first-gen. People connect with someone who is honest about where they started and how hard they're working to get somewhere.

**Scarcity:** Position myself as someone with a rare mix: technical skill plus the drive that comes from building my own path. Employers see a lot of IT resumes, but fewer candidates with that story behind them.

**Unity:** Connect with other first-gen students and NJIT IT students. "We" came up the hard way together, and that shared identity builds a strong network.
